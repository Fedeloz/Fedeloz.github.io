Open-World, Communication-Aware Multi-Robot Exploration with Vision Language Models

Federico Lozano-Cuadra1, Beatriz Soret1, Marc Sanchez Net2, Federico Rossi2, and Abhishek Cauligi3

1 Telecommunications Research Institute, University of Málaga, Spain

2 Jet Propulsion Laboratory, California Institute of Technology, Pasadena, CA USA

3 Department of Mechanical Engineering, Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore, MD USA

Abstract

Multi-robot systems (MRS) will play an increasingly greater role in future robot operations, particularly in open-world environments typical of disaster recovery and planetary exploration. Planning for such MRS remains a formidable problem: coordination of multiple intelligent agents introduces novel challenges, in particular a large action space that increases combinatorially with the number of agents as well as unreliable inter-agent communication in open-world environments. Recently, vision language models (VLMs) have emerged as a promising paradigm for robot planning; VLMs allow users to flexibly express task specifications and semantic considerations through multi-modal interfaces, including natural language. Yet, the use of VLMs for planning and coordination of MRS remains an active area of research.

We propose an architecture in which general-purpose VLMs jointly guide mobility and communication decisions of autonomous robotic agents, consuming the robots’ sensor streams; deciding which information should be shared among the agents; and issuing commands to an autonomous mobility stack. We demonstrate that grounding VLM decisions in role-specific contexts with local observations and dynamic persistent memory leads to dramatically improved task performance and scaling.

We validate our framework through extensive simulations of a multi-agent mapping task, wherein a team of agents explores an unmapped region and task performance is measured by map reconstruction at a base station. Experiments show that the proposed architecture improves data packet delivery ratio and exploration coverage by up to 30.6% and 8.3%, respectively, compared to the strongest baseline, while demonstrating good scalability with increasing map size. We further provide design insights and recommendations through ablation studies and model comparisons within our proposed architecture.
