Learning Decentralized, Mission-Aware Communications for Autonomous Lunar Rover Swarms

Federico Lozano-Cuadra1, Beatriz Soret1, Marc Sanchez Net2, Abhishek Cauligi3, and Federico Rossi2

1 Telecommunications Research Institute, University of Málaga, Spain

2 Jet Propulsion Laboratory, California Institute of Technology, Pasadena, CA USA

3 Department of Mechanical Engineering, Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore, MD USA

Abstract

Future lunar exploration missions will rely on autonomous rover swarms operating beyond continuous lander coverage, under intermittent connectivity and severe communication, energy, and storage constraints. In such settings, rover networks naturally operate as lunar delay-tolerant networks (LDTNs), requiring decentralized and mission-aware communication strategies.

We propose a mission-aware framework that models exploration, sensing and data generation, and physical and communication constraints, using parameters and relevant scenarios from NASA’s Cooperative Autonomous Distributed Robotic Exploration (CADRE) mission. Exploration and communication are coupled, since the rovers’ trajectories determine when they can communicate. Within this framework, routing is performed by a fully decentralized and scalable policy based on graph attention-based multi-agent reinforcement learning (GAT-MARL). Two mission-aware mechanisms complement the routing: content-aware queue ordering, which prioritizes the data most relevant to the mission objectives, and a relay mode in which idle rovers support the swarm connectivity.

Monte Carlo packet-level simulations in representative lunar environments show that GAT-MARL consistently outperforms classical delay-tolerant network routing baselines. Notably, the integrated GAT-MARL with the mission-aware mechanisms enable a swarm of only 5 rovers to achieve a mean reported area comparable to that of a 15-rover swarm with the best classical routing approach (98.09% vs. 97.87%, respectively), demonstrating that intelligent, mission-aware decentralized communication can substantially reduce the required swarm size for a given mission objective. Furthermore the model is lightweight, with estimated microsecond-scale inference time on CADRE-class hardware.
